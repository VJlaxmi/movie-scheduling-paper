\begin{abstract}
The Movie Scene Scheduling Problem (MSSP) seeks to sequence film scenes across shooting days to minimize total production cost and makespan, subject to a rich set of real-world constraints including actor availability, hierarchical location logistics, crew travel continuity, labor regulations, and deadline penalties.
Existing approaches either solve simplified MSSP variants exactly via mixed-integer linear programming (MILP), which becomes intractable beyond 15--20 scenes, or apply standalone metaheuristics that ignore exploitable problem structure.
We first present a comprehensive MILP formulation with \textbf{19 constraint families} over a three-tier geographic hierarchy (city--town--location), actor--scene--day and location--scene--day linking variables, town-uniqueness and town-continuity logistics constraints, equipment sharing, booking/holding fees, co-scheduling, scene separation, location concurrency, lead-actor rest periods, criticality-weighted utilization, and consecutive working-day limits---substantially extending prior formulations.
We then propose \textbf{BDM-AOS}, a Bi-level Decomposition Matheuristic with Adaptive Operator Selection.
Our method introduces:
(i)~a \emph{Scene Interaction Graph} (SIG) that captures actor-sharing, location-sharing, and transfer-cost coupling between scenes, enabling Louvain community detection to partition scenes into tightly coupled \emph{shooting blocks};
(ii)~a bi-level framework where NSGA-II evolves block permutations at the upper level while a global greedy decoder schedules scenes in block-guided order, simultaneously optimizing cost and makespan;
(iii)~a Q-learning adaptive operator selection mechanism that dynamically chooses among standard and two novel problem-specific operators---Location-Aware Crossover (LAX) and Actor-Continuity Mutation (ACM).
Comprehensive experiments on 8 synthetic instance sizes (10--100 scenes) with 30 independent seeds per algorithm reveal a scalability crossover: while GA and PSO outperform BDM-AOS on small instances ($\leq 30$ scenes), BDM-AOS achieves \textbf{20.7\% lower cost} than GA and \textbf{19.0\% lower cost} than PSO on 75-scene instances, with a \textbf{5.6$\times$ runtime speedup}.
On the largest 100-scene instances, BDM-AOS maintains a \textbf{14.5\%} cost advantage over GA.
A five-variant ablation study identifies SIG decomposition as the critical component (33--64\% degradation without it).
Evaluation uses hypervolume (HV) and inverted generational distance (IGD) quality indicators, with Friedman tests and Holm's post-hoc correction for statistical rigor.
All code and data are publicly available at \url{https://github.com/VJlaxmi/movie-scheduling-paper}.
\end{abstract}
