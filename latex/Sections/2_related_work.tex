\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Exact Methods for the MSSP}

The MSSP was first formalized as an integer linear program by \citet{zhen2014}, who minimized actor wages and transfer costs using CPLEX.
\citet{liu2019} extended this ILP with 24 constraints covering actor availability, location compatibility, and scene-day assignments, solving instances up to 15 scenes optimally before CPLEX exceeded 7{,}200-second time limits.
\citet{tekin2023} developed the most comprehensive MILP formulation to date, with 31 constraints including precedence relations, time windows, and actor blocking periods.
Using CPLEX on 160 benchmark instances, Tekin showed that exact solutions become impractical beyond 25 scenes with blocking-time constraints, motivating the need for metaheuristic approaches.

\subsection{Metaheuristic Approaches}

\citet{liu2019} introduced a Tabu Search-based metaheuristic (TSBM) achieving average gaps of 0.18\% from optimal on instances with 10--40 scenes and 3--20 actors, along with a PSO-based method (PSOBM) with 7.92\% average gaps.
\citet{long2020} added an Ant Colony Optimization method (ACOBM) and showed ACO outperformed both TSBM and PSOBM in solution quality on larger instances.
\citet{tekin2023} developed a discrete PSO with binary position representation and feasibility-based repair, achieving less than 1\% deviation from optimal on 106 out of 160 instances and finding better solutions than CPLEX (which timed out) on a real film script with 70 scenes and 43 actors.

All existing metaheuristics operate on flat scene permutations without exploiting the coupling structure between scenes.
None employ multi-objective optimization, decomposition strategies, or adaptive operator selection.

\subsection{Multi-Objective Scheduling}

Multi-objective evolutionary algorithms have been successfully applied to related scheduling problems.
\citet{deb2002} introduced NSGA-II, which remains the standard for bi-objective optimization.
\citet{han2025} applied NSGA-III to film scene visual optimization (a different problem from MSSP) with Wilcoxon rank-sum and Friedman statistical testing, demonstrating the viability of multi-objective evolutionary approaches in film production contexts.

\subsection{Matheuristics and Adaptive Operator Selection}

Matheuristics---hybrids of mathematical programming and metaheuristics---have shown strong performance on combinatorial optimization problems~\citep{talbi2009, maniezzo2010}.
Decomposition-based matheuristics solve sub-problems exactly while using metaheuristics for global coordination.
To our knowledge, no matheuristic has been applied to the MSSP.

Adaptive operator selection (AOS) uses online learning to choose effective operators during search.
\citet{thierens2005} introduced credit assignment for operator selection.
Q-learning-based AOS~\citep{li2014, fialho2008} adapts operator probabilities based on search progress, population diversity, and improvement rate.
