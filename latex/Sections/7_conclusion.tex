\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented BDM-AOS, a bi-level decomposition approach with adaptive operator selection for the Movie Scene Scheduling Problem.
Our work makes three contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item The \emph{Scene Interaction Graph} decomposes scenes into shooting blocks based on actor-sharing, location-sharing, and transfer cost structure, reducing the search space from $n!$ to $K!$ permutations.
    
    \item The \emph{bi-level framework} combines NSGA-II over block permutations with a global greedy decoder. The decomposition guides scene ordering while cross-block day sharing preserves cost-competitiveness.
    
    \item \emph{Q-learning adaptive operator selection} with two problem-specific operators (LAX and ACM) adapts the search strategy, yielding a 1.4\% cost reduction over random operator selection on the S25 instance.
\end{enumerate}

Experiments on instances with 10--25 scenes show that BDM-AOS achieves cost within 1.6\% of GA on S10 and within 0.4\% of PSO on S25.
On instances where Louvain produces few blocks ($K \leq 4$), the block-permutation space is too small and GA/PSO outperform BDM significantly.
As $K$ grows (e.g., $K=6$ on S25), the gap narrows, suggesting the approach would benefit from larger instances.

The bi-objective formulation revealed that cost and makespan do not conflict under greedy decoding---a methodological finding that motivates alternative decoder designs in future work.

\subsection{Future Work}

Several promising directions emerge from this work:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Real-world validation:} Apply BDM-AOS to real film scripts (e.g., the 70-scene, 43-actor instance from~\citet{tekin2023}) to validate practical applicability.
    
    \item \textbf{Adaptive decomposition:} Re-cluster the SIG periodically during search to exploit evolving population structure.
    
    \item \textbf{Uncertainty handling:} Incorporate stochastic elements (weather delays, actor illness) via robust or stochastic programming at the sub-problem level.
    
    \item \textbf{Deep RL for AOS:} Replace tabular Q-learning with deep Q-networks for continuous state representation and finer-grained operator selection.
    
    \item \textbf{Many-objective extension:} Extend to three or more objectives (cost, makespan, actor fatigue, location utilization) using NSGA-III.
    
    \item \textbf{Parallel implementation:} Evaluate blocks concurrently to reduce wall-clock time for large instances.
\end{itemize}
