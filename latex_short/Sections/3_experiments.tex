\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Setup}

\paragraph{Instances.}
Eight synthetic configurations spanning 10--100 scenes (Table~\ref{tab:instances}), generated with fixed seed~42.
Parameters follow literature ranges~\citep{bomsdorf2008}: wages \$80--\$1{,}000/day, 20\% precedence probability, 80\% actor availability, hierarchical transfer costs (\$100--\$8{,}000 by tier).
All 19 constraint families are active in every instance.

\paragraph{Algorithms.}
\textbf{BDM-AOS} (population~20, generations~40, $p_c{=}0.9$, $p_m{=}0.3$);
\textbf{GA} (population~50, generations~100, OX crossover, $p_c{=}0.85$, $p_m{=}0.15$);
\textbf{PSO} (swarm~30, iterations~100, $w{=}0.7$, $c_1{=}c_2{=}1.5$);
\textbf{MILP} (CBC solver, 300s limit).
Each metaheuristic is run with 30 independent seeds.
Five ablation variants isolate BDM components: BDM-NoAOS, BDM-NoSpecOps, BDM-Flat, BDM-NoSIG.

\paragraph{MILP comparison.}
BDM-AOS matches MILP optimality on S10 (both \$12.5k, $19\times$ faster at 0.20s vs.\ 3.8s) and is within 4\% on S15 ($129\times$ faster).
For $n \geq 20$, MILP exhausts its 300s budget without proving optimality (integrality gap 22--39\%); for $n \geq 30$, MILP finds no feasible solution at all.
BDM-AOS solves every instance in under 2 seconds regardless of scale---a $>\!186\times$ runtime advantage at S100.

Results (Table~\ref{tab:results}, Figure~\ref{fig:scalability}) follow a clear scalability crossover (block counts $K$ in Table~\ref{tab:instances}).
On small--medium instances (S10--S30, $K{=}3$--$8$), GA and PSO outperform BDM-AOS: the block space is too coarse relative to the scene space.
At S50 ($K{=}14$), the gap narrows to 5.6\%.
At S75 ($K{=}17$), BDM-AOS \textbf{outperforms both baselines by 20.7\% and 19.0\%} while running $5.6\times$ faster.
At S100 ($K{=}26$), BDM-AOS maintains a \textbf{14.5\%} cost advantage over GA at $5.5\times$ speedup.

Wilcoxon rank-sum tests (Holm-corrected, $\alpha{=}0.05$): on S75 and S100, BDM-AOS significantly outperforms both GA and PSO ($p < 0.001$).

\textbf{SIG decomposition is the critical component}: BDM-NoSIG (random blocks) degrades by 33\% on S50, 64\% on S75, and 41\% on S100---confirming that Louvain-guided partitioning captures genuine actor--location coupling.
\textbf{Q-learning AOS} provides modest, scale-dependent gains (1.7\%--2.0\% on S50--S75), diminishing at S100 as the Q-table state discretization becomes too coarse for $K{=}26$ blocks.
\textbf{Domain-specific operators} (LAX/ACM) show a striking S75 spike (+30.4\%), where block count $K{=}17$ aligns well with operator design assumptions; this effect diminishes at S100.
\textbf{BDM-Flat} confirms that intermediate block count is optimal: operating at $K{=}n$ causes 8.6\%--59.9\% degradation on S50--S75.
